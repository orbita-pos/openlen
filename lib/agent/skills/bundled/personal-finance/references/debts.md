# Debts and goals

## The list
For each debt: who it's with, the balance, the interest rate (APR), the minimum payment, and whether the rate is fixed.
A debt whose rate they don't know: ask them to find it on the contract or statement, and mark it `[VERIFY]` until then.

## Two orders
Pay the minimum on all of them, and put every extra amount on one:
- **Avalanche**: the highest interest rate first. Pays the least interest overall.
- **Snowball**: the smallest balance first. Each one gone frees its minimum for the next, and the quick wins keep people
  going.
Show both with their numbers (months to finish, interest paid) and let them choose. When a payment is cleared, its
amount rolls onto the next debt.

## Months to pay off one debt
With balance B, monthly rate r (APR / 12) and payment P:
months = −ln(1 − r·B / P) / ln(1 + r). If P ≤ r·B the debt never shrinks: say so plainly — the payment doesn't even cover
the interest. Check the result by simulating month by month in a table when it matters.

## Warning signs to name
- Paying only minimums on cards with high interest.
- A new loan to pay an old one, unless the new rate is clearly lower and the fees are counted.
- Missed payments: late fees and the credit record. Suggest they call the lender before missing one; many have hardship
  plans.
- Debt that can't be paid in any order: say that a non-profit debt adviser in their country can help, and that it isn't
  a failure.

## Goals
For each goal: amount, date, months left, and monthly amount = (amount − saved) / months left. If the sum of goals
doesn't fit what's left, order them with the person and move dates rather than starve the buffer.

## The emergency fund
1. First a small buffer: about one month of essential costs.
2. Then the debts above.
3. Then grow the buffer toward three to six months of essential costs, more with irregular income.
Kept somewhere they can reach in a day, apart from the everyday account.
