# The budget

## From their figures
1. **Income**: what actually arrives each month, after tax. Irregular income: use the lowest of the last three to six
   months, and treat the rest as extra.
2. **Fixed costs**: the same every month — rent or mortgage, bills, insurance, subscriptions, minimum loan payments.
3. **Variable costs**: food, transport, going out, clothes. From a statement if they have one (sum by category); if
   not, their estimate, marked as an estimate until a month of real figures replaces it.
4. **What's left** = income − fixed − variable. Negative means the month doesn't close: that comes first.

## Finding money
- Look for the few big items, not twenty small ones: housing, transport, food and subscriptions are usually where it is.
- Subscriptions and memberships they no longer use are the easiest win: list them and let them choose.
- Give two or three options with what each frees a month; they pick.

## Simple rules people know
- **50/30/20**: needs about 50 %, wants about 30 %, saving and extra debt payments about 20 %. A rough guide, not a
  goal: with high rent it won't fit, and that's fine.
- **Zero-based**: every unit of income gets a job (a cost, a goal or the buffer) until "left" is zero.
Offer one if they want a rule; their own numbers matter more than the rule.

## Each month
Compare what they planned with what happened, per category, in one table. Say the one category that went most over and
ask whether to adjust the plan or the spending.
