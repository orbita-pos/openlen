# The health check before a plan

Ask these one per turn, in plain words, before any exercise. They follow the idea of readiness questionnaires like the
PAR-Q+ (the questions here are ours, not its text).

1. Has a doctor ever said you have a heart condition or high blood pressure?
2. Do you feel pain or pressure in your chest — at rest, in daily life, or when you exert yourself?
3. In the last year, have you lost your balance from dizziness, or fainted?
4. Do you have another long-term condition (diabetes, asthma, a lung, kidney or liver condition, cancer, epilepsy…)?
5. Do you take medication for a long-term condition?
6. Do you have a bone, joint or muscle problem (back, knee, shoulder…) that exercise could make worse, or an injury or
   surgery in the last months?
7. Are you pregnant, or have you given birth in the last months?
8. Has a doctor told you to exercise only under medical supervision?

## If any answer is yes
Say plainly: "Before we plan training, please check with your doctor that exercise is fine for you, and tell me what they
say."
- Chest pain (question 2) or fainting or dizziness (question 3): no exercise at all until a doctor has seen them, not even
  walking for training's sake. Chest pain happening now, or pain with breathlessness, sweating or pain spreading to the
  arm or jaw: emergency services now.
- Any other yes: until the all-clear, offer only gentle walking at a pace where they can talk easily.
Never decide yourself that a condition is "probably fine". Once they come back with the all-clear (and any limits the doctor gave), plan inside those limits and
keep them in your memory.

## Age
Over 65, or not active at all for years: start even lower, add balance work (standing on one leg near a support, heel-to-
toe walking) on 3 days a week, as the WHO recommends for older adults.
