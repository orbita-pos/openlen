# Programming the training

## How much (WHO 2020 guidelines on physical activity, adults 18–64)
- Aerobic: 150–300 minutes a week of moderate activity, or 75–150 of vigorous, or a mix.
- Muscle strengthening at moderate or greater intensity, all major muscle groups, on 2 or more days a week.
- Less sitting; any activity is better than none.
- Over 65: the same, plus balance and strength together (multicomponent activity) on 3 or more days.
Source: World Health Organization, *WHO guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour* (2020),
who.int/publications/i/item/9789240015128.

## How hard
- **Talk test**: moderate = they can talk but not sing; vigorous = only a few words at a time.
- **Effort 1–10**: 1 is sitting, 10 is all-out. Most beginner work sits at 5–7. Strength sets end with 2–3 reps they
  could still have done.

## How it grows (progressive overload)
Change one thing at a time, in this order: reps → sets → load or pace. About 5–10 % more a week at most. If a session
felt 8 or more for two weeks running, hold. Every 4th to 6th week is lighter (about half the volume) so the body catches
up.

## Shapes that work
- **Beginner strength** (2–3 days, a day of rest between): squat or sit-to-stand, push-up (wall or knees first), a row
  or band pull, a hinge (hip bridge, then deadlift with light weight), a carry or plank. 2–3 sets of 8–12.
- **From walking to running a 5 km** (3 days a week, 8–10 weeks): alternate running and walking, from 1 min running /
  2 min walking toward 30 minutes of running; repeat a week whenever one felt too hard.
- **Every session**: 5–10 minutes of warm-up (easy movement, then the session's movements light), the main part, 5
  minutes of cool-down.

## Rest
At least one full day off a week; two hard sessions for the same muscles never on consecutive days. Sleep is part of the
plan.
