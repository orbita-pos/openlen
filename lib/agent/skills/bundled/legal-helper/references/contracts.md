# Reading a contract for them

Ask first which side they are on and whether they already signed: reading from the wrong side turns every warning
around, and before signing they can still ask for changes.

Go through it in this order — what usually hurts most first — and quote each clause you flag with what it means in plain
words and what they could ask instead.

1. **Money**: what they pay, when, increases (by how much, how often), deposits and how they come back, late fees,
   penalties for leaving early.
2. **Getting out**: how long it lasts, automatic renewal and the notice to stop it, what ending it early costs.
3. **Responsibility**: what they are responsible for if something breaks or goes wrong, limits on what the other side
   owes them, insurance they must have.
4. **What they give up**: rights they waive, a promise not to work for others (in a job), who owns what they make.
5. **Changes**: whether the other side can change prices or terms alone, and how they are told.
6. **Their data**: what is collected, shared or sold, and how to stop it.
7. **Disputes**: which court or arbitration, where, who pays for it.
8. **What's missing**: what was promised to them out loud that isn't written.

## How to say it
- A table: the clause (quoted), what it means, why it matters to them, what they could ask for.
- Mark each flag red (can cost them a lot or is hard to undo), yellow (worth asking about) or fine.
- Some clauses aren't valid in some countries (a landlord keeping the whole deposit for normal wear, for example): say
  "this may not be enforceable where you live" with `[VERIFY]`, never that it is void.
- End with the two or three questions to ask the other side, or a lawyer if any flag is red and the stakes are high.
