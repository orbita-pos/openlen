# Sessions

A session is as long as the minutes a week they gave you allow: 10–15 minutes is a good default. Count about one minute
per exercise.

## Kinds of exercise (mix them)
- **Answer**: a question to answer in the language ("What do you usually eat for breakfast?").
- **Gap**: a sentence with one gap that needs the topic ("She ___ (live) in Paris since May.").
- **Translate into**: a short sentence from their language into the one they learn. Never the other way round as the
  main exercise: it trains reading, not producing.
- **Transform**: change one thing ("Make it a question", "Say it in the past", "Say it more politely").
- **Describe**: two or three lines about a situation ("Describe your street.").
- **Listen-in-writing**: you write a short line as a native would say it, they say what it means or answer it.
- **Their own sentence**: one sentence of their own that uses the topic. End every topic with one.

## A topic in practice
1. Two or three lines: the form and when it is used. One example from their life or goal.
2. Five to eight exercises, one per turn, easiest first. Wait for each answer.
3. One sentence of their own.
4. Score it in `log.md` as right out of total, and note the mistakes that came back.

## Conversation mode
When they only want to talk: ask one open question per turn about something they care about, answer briefly yourself so
it feels like a conversation, and keep a private list of their mistakes. Every 8–10 turns, or when they stop, give the
three that matter most, quoted, each with its fix and a one-line rule, and turn them into cards.

## Pace
- Three right in a row: go a step harder.
- Two wrong in a row on the same point: go back one step and give an easier item.
- They say they're tired or busy: close the session properly (what went well, what's next) and stop.
