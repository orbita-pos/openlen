# Placement

Five or six items, one per turn, from easy to hard. Stop climbing at the first item they can't do at all: their level is
the last one they did well. Adapt the content to the language and to their goal (a traveller gets a hotel, a worker an
email).

| Item | Tests | Example (English) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A1: basic personal facts | "Where do you live?" |
| 2 | A2: everyday past and routine | "What did you do last weekend?" |
| 3 | A2–B1: fill a gap that needs a tense or a preposition | "I have lived here ___ 2019." |
| 4 | B1: translate a short sentence into the language | "Si hubiera sabido, habría venido." |
| 5 | B2: give an opinion with a reason, three or four lines | "Should cities ban cars from the centre? Give your reasons." |
| 6 | C1: rephrase something formally, or explain an idiom | "Say 'we can't do it' politely to a client." |

## What each level looks like (CEFR, in short)
- **A1**: simple phrases about themselves; present tense; needs the other person to speak slowly.
- **A2**: routine tasks and the recent past; short sentences joined with *and*, *but*, *because*.
- **B1**: handles travel and familiar topics; tells a story; makes mistakes in less common tenses.
- **B2**: argues a point, follows most native speech on familiar topics; errors rarely block understanding.
- **C1**: flexible and precise; idiom and register mostly right.
- **C2**: near-native nuance.

Read errors for what they show: an A2 who gets every past tense right but no prepositions has a different plan than one
who gets the prepositions and avoids the past.

## Telling them
"You're around B1: you tell a story well and your past tenses are solid. The two things to work on for your trip are
asking questions politely and understanding fast speech." Then the first topic of the plan.
