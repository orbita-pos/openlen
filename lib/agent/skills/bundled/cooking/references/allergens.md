# Allergens

The official lists of the allergens that must be declared on food labels. Someone can be allergic to something not on
them: what the person tells you is what counts.

## European Union — 14 (Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011, Annex II; eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2011/1169/oj)
1. Cereals containing gluten (wheat, rye, barley, oats, spelt…)
2. Crustaceans
3. Eggs
4. Fish
5. Peanuts
6. Soybeans
7. Milk (including lactose)
8. Tree nuts (almonds, hazelnuts, walnuts, cashews, pecans, Brazil nuts, pistachios, macadamias)
9. Celery
10. Mustard
11. Sesame seeds
12. Sulphur dioxide and sulphites (above 10 mg/kg or 10 mg/litre)
13. Lupin
14. Molluscs

## United States — 9 major food allergens (FDA: the Food Allergen Labeling and Consumer Protection Act, plus sesame by the
FASTER Act from 2023; fda.gov/food/food-labeling-nutrition/food-allergies)
Milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans, sesame.

## Where they hide
- Gluten: soy sauce (use tamari labelled gluten-free), stock cubes, breadcrumbs, beer, some spice mixes.
- Milk: butter, some breads, "non-dairy" creamers with caseinate.
- Eggs: fresh pasta, mayonnaise, some glazes.
- Nuts and peanuts: pesto, sauces of Asian cooking, cereal bars, some oils.
- Fish and crustaceans: Worcestershire sauce, fish sauce, shrimp paste.
- Sesame: tahini, hummus, some breads and dressings.
- Celery and mustard: stocks, soups, sauces, spice mixes.
- Sulphites: dried fruit, wine, vinegar.

## Swaps
Egg in baking: a tablespoon of ground flax with three of water per egg. Milk: an oat or soy drink (soy is an allergen
too; for someone avoiding gluten, only an oat drink labelled gluten-free, as oats are often contaminated with wheat). Wheat flour: a gluten-free blend (results differ: say so). Peanuts in a sauce: sunflower seed butter.
