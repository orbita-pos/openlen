# Food safety

From the U.S. Department of Agriculture's Food Safety and Inspection Service (USDA FSIS), *Safe Minimum Internal
Temperature Chart* and its basics of safe handling: fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/safe-temperature-chart.
Measured with a food thermometer in the thickest part, away from bone. Rounded to whole degrees Celsius.

| Food | Safe minimum internal temperature |
|---|---|
| Poultry, whole or in pieces, and ground poultry | 165 °F / 74 °C |
| Ground meat (beef, pork, veal, lamb) | 160 °F / 71 °C |
| Beef, pork, veal, lamb — steaks, chops, roasts | 145 °F / 63 °C, then a 3-minute rest |
| Fish and shellfish | 145 °F / 63 °C (or until the flesh is opaque and flakes) |
| Egg dishes | 160 °F / 71 °C; eggs until white and yolk are firm |
| Leftovers and casseroles, reheated | 165 °F / 74 °C |

## Keeping food safe
- The "danger zone" is 40–140 °F (4–60 °C): bacteria grow fastest there.
- Cooked or perishable food goes into the fridge within 2 hours (1 hour when it is above 90 °F / 32 °C outside).
- Fridge at 40 °F / 4 °C or below; freezer at 0 °F / −18 °C.
- Leftovers: 3 to 4 days in the fridge; frozen, they keep safe longer but lose quality.
- Thaw in the fridge, in cold water (changed every 30 minutes), or in the microwave and cook at once — never on the
  counter.
- Raw meat, poultry and fish never touch what is eaten raw: separate boards, wash hands and surfaces after.
