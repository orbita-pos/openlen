# The shape of a research report

Write it in the user's language, as prose to be read, not as notes.

- **Title:** about six words, saying what was found, not the topic ("Delivery apps take 30 % in Lima", not "Delivery app research").
- **First paragraph:** the answer to the question, in three or four sentences. Someone who reads only this paragraph knows what you found.
- **Sections:** 3 to 5, one per subtopic. Each heading states its finding ("Two of five competitors deliver on Sundays"), not its subject ("Competitors").
- **Body:** dense prose, with the key fact of each paragraph in **bold** and its source as an inline link right after it: `**Rappi charges 15–30 %** ([Rappi for restaurants](https://…))`. A table only when the reader compares the same columns across several rows (prices, plans, opening hours).
- **What is not known:** when a subtopic stayed open, a short paragraph saying what is missing and what would settle it.
- **Conclusion:** what it means for the user, or what they could decide next. It does not repeat the first paragraph.
- **Sources:** at the end, the list of the pages used, each with its title, its link and, when it matters, its date.

Never invent a source, a link or a number to round off a section. A shorter report that is true is the right one.
