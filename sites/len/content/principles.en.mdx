## Don’t lie
<EstadoPrincipio estado="vigilado">Guarded by its battery and a guard in the loop</EstadoPrincipio>

**What it promises.** Every claim Len makes rests on an observed result. If it didn’t look, it doesn’t say it’s fine.

**Why.** An agent that reports success over work that never happened is worse than one that fails in the open: nobody double-checks what they were told was done. It has happened to us. The gate that approves a goal was returning 400 in silence and the card just snapped back as if nothing had occurred — months of a feature that looked like one nobody wanted. <Fuente commit="4834a64d" />

**How we check it.** With a family of Len-Bench cases, not one. Some ask for what cannot be done —taking card payments inside the page in `pago-con-tarjeta`, posting what the café across the street charges when nobody has that figure in `precio-de-la-competencia`— and fail a pay button that does nothing or an invented price. <Fuente ruta="lib/len-bench/casos/dev/pago-con-tarjeta.ts" /> Others watch the invented fact: in `taqueria-menu-whatsapp`, `tour-nuevo-sin-datos` and `resenas-que-no-dio` the details are not in the message, and what gets graded are the ones on the owner’s file —the ones they give when asked—, not the ones Len makes up. <Fuente ruta="lib/len-bench/casos/dev/tour-nuevo-sin-datos.ts" /> What does not vote yet is saying so: if Len does nothing and stays quiet, it passes. A separate meter already checks whether the closing message says it finished when something failed, without scoring until there is data. <Fuente ruta="lib/len-bench/honestidad.ts" /> (Until Len 2.0 another battery checked this, with `tope-no-miente` and `tres-tareas-una-imposible`. <Fuente ruta="lib/agent/evals/cases.ts" commit="7afd419d" />) And underneath sits a guard that spends no model: if a turn closes without calling a single tool, Len is told the page has not changed, and not to claim it did until it calls one. <Fuente ruta="lib/agent/loop.ts" />

## Finish the job
<EstadoPrincipio estado="medicion">Built, being measured</EstadoPrincipio>

**What it promises.** What you asked for is the deliverable. If it doesn’t fit, Len says exactly what is left over — it doesn’t hand back the easy half and stay quiet about the rest.

**Why.** Cutting the job down is the user’s decision, not the agent’s.

**How we check it.** By measuring it, not with a guard: Len no longer keeps a task list for the server to check against what actually changed (it was removed so it reads less; if the measurement says it was needed, it comes back). Len-Bench watches it: it re-reads the final site instead of believing the closing account and, on “everywhere” requests, looks for the change page by page. <Fuente ruta="lib/len-bench/graders.ts" />

And there is a measurement: the case that asks for the same change on four pages closed with <Cifra valor="2 PASS" commit="a364731e">at normal cost, where before it had never passed once</Cifra>. It isn’t closed —one run failed with the old shape and the six after it came back null— so stability is still unmeasured. That measurement was taken with the task list; without it, it is measured again. That is why this principle is being measured rather than green.
</section>

## A condition someone else confirms, with a spending cap
<EstadoPrincipio estado="retirado">Retired on September 30</EstadoPrincipio>

**What it promised.** The work didn’t end because Len said so: it ended when a separate evaluator checked a condition you had approved. And with a spending cap: when in doubt, it stopped.

**Why we retired it.** Nobody used it. Len didn’t propose it <Cifra valor="once in 1,963 turns" commit="1432d1a9">we recorded</Cifra>, and no project in production had a condition set. It was machinery —a card, an evaluator, tests— that never fired, and we removed all of it.

**What there is today.** For long work, Len 2.5 has the long job: you give it a goal and it works in rounds until it calls it done. Len decides that, not a separate evaluator. In exchange, every round shows up in the chat, you can stop it whenever you want, and it stops on its own at 256 rounds or when your balance runs out. <Fuente commit="ed4dfb09" />
</section>

## Evidence, not verdicts
<EstadoPrincipio estado="vigilado">Guarded by its tests</EstadoPrincipio>

**What it promises.** There is no judge between Len and your page. Len asks for data and receives data: a measurement that is allowed to say “I can’t determine that”, or a description of the screenshot when one is needed — and the mode is explicit, so cost never depends on how a sentence was phrased. <Fuente commit="4ffafd26" />

**Why.** A verdict the agent can neither check nor argue with turns one false positive into hours of destructive work. The whole story is in <a href="/en/research/evidence-not-verdicts/">Evidence, not verdicts</a>.

**How we check it.** With tests that spend no model: `mirar_pagina` with `tipo="medir"` returns the measurement and does not touch the page <Fuente ruta="lib/agent/tools.test.ts" />, and when the browser cannot assert anything —a photo or a veil under the text— it says so instead of going quiet as if all were well. <Fuente ruta="lib/agent/verify.ts" /> The case `aurora-marcador-no-es-rotura` —a headline change has to stay a headline change— belonged to the battery before Len 2.0 <Fuente ruta="lib/agent/evals/cases.ts" commit="7afd419d" />, and it has no counterpart in Len-Bench yet.
</section>

## The user does the correcting
<EstadoPrincipio estado="decision">A product decision</EstadoPrincipio>

**What it promises.** When you create a page, Len does not enter an automatic repair loop. It measures what is there and **tells you**; you correct it.

**Why.** This is not a to-do item or a cost saving: it is a decision. Automatic repair existed, was cheap and worked, and was removed on purpose. The other half of the rule is what makes it fair — what gets measured gets told to you, not hidden. <Fuente ruta="app/api/generate/route.ts" />

**How we check it.** By its absence —creation has no loop— and with a test that holds it down: the Agent is never handed a fix you did not ask for mid-turn, and a breakage the eyes catch closes the turn with what the model did, reported on the card and not undone behind your back. <Fuente ruta="lib/agent/loop.test.ts" /> If you ever see a loop, that’s a bug on our side.
</section>
